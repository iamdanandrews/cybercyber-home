Manifesto — what we believe
Ten beliefs
Judgement · Refusal
Est. London

Ten things
we believe.

Anyone can build it now. Almost nobody knows if they should. What a product design lab believes about judgement, refusal, and what's worth building — rather than just shipping fast.

01
Anyone can build it now. Almost nobody knows if they should.

Execution stopped being the hard part. Deciding what deserves to exist, what to cut, and what the thing should actually do — that's where products are won and lost. It doesn't come from a model or a process. It comes from having been wrong enough times to see it early.

02
We don't write decks.

Decks are how studios survive without shipping. If we have an idea, we build it. If we can't build it yet, we show you the closest working version we can make this week.

03
We don't worship the velocity gods.

The industry started worshipping speed for its own sake — ship faster, ship more, never ask what you're shipping. The result is a market drowning in technically-functional products nobody remembers. The hard part was never shipping. It was knowing what's worth building.

04
The version worth keeping is the only version worth shipping.

Most products shipped this year won't survive it. We build the ones still in your customers' hands later. Fast, yes — but fast at the right thing. Better in a way that compounds.

05
We work with a small number of clients each year.

We turn down more work than we take. Not because we're precious — because the math of doing this work properly only holds at small scale.

06
The price isn't the conversation.

We charge what the work is worth. No discounts, no bundles. If our pricing feels like a lot, we're probably not the right lab for you. If it feels like a bargain, you're paying attention.

07
AI is a tool, not the product.

We build things that happen to use AI, not AI things that happen to be products. The distinction matters. Buyers can tell the difference, even when they can't articulate it. Their customers definitely can.

08
We build in public.

You'll see the work as it's being made. You'll see what shipped last week, and what got killed for not being worth keeping. Hiding the process is how studios protect mediocrity. We don't have any to protect.

09
The medium keeps moving. The bar doesn't.

Screens, agents, glasses, whatever's next — the interface changes every few years and most people chase it. We don't. We bring the same standard to whatever comes after the screen. The frontier isn't a trend to catch. It's the next place to do the work properly.

10
Build the mechanism, not the output.

Most work is sold as a stream — assets this month, more next month, forever. Sometimes the right answer is a system that generates the work and hands you the keys, even when that means we're not needed again. The judgement goes in once, at the design layer, and then it compounds without us.
