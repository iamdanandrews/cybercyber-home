04 / Case study

A session ends.
The record grows.

A native jiu-jitsu journal connecting the moment of capture to a personal record of training. Identity and a product loop designed together.

Scope
Identity · Interaction design · Native iOS
Project
Owned product · Alpha · Dan Andrews · 2026
The brief

Saving a session is the beginning, not the value.

A useful training journal needs to make the record worth returning to. I designed and built Frame around capture, organisation and return: a short account of training becomes a session record, techniques can be found by position, and recall brings material back between sessions.

The defining decision

Separate the effort of remembering from the work of organising. A small entry becomes a record with more than one way back in.

The product in use

Two ways back into training.

Move from training history into the Game view, then inspect the notes and recall records attached to a position. The recording follows two ways of finding a practitioner’s past experience.

01 / CaptureFrame

Start with what the practitioner remembers.

After training, a practitioner has a partial account of a session. Frame starts with that account rather than asking for a complete technique taxonomy before anything can be saved.

The session keeps the practitioner’s reflection alongside the rounds, positions and submissions. A partial memory becomes a structured record without losing the words that make it personal.

02 / FindFrame

Time and position are different ways back in.

The Log answers “What did I train?” The Game view answers “What do I know from this position?” The native screens organise the same practice through two different structures.

The position-based library gives techniques a place beyond the session in which they were first recorded. Neither route has to carry both navigation tasks.

03 / ReturnFrame

The record should earn a return visit.

Inside each position, technique notes sit beside confidence, rolling history and recall. The record brings a specific move back into focus, with the context of what the practitioner has done with it.

The native Closed Guard view makes that relationship visible. Its seeded statistics demonstrate the interaction; improved recall and lasting training habits still need evidence from use.
