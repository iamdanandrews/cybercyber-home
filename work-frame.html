The work
02 / FRAME

Frame is a BJJ training journal built on a hard truth: you forget most of what you’re taught within days. Log a session in seconds by voice, and Frame holds every technique, brings it back just before you’d forget it, and shows you — in plain data — that you’re actually improving. Built for the belts who feel stuck, so they have a reason to keep turning up.

Built against the forgetting curve

A single session throws four or five details at you — the grip, the angle, the small correction that made the sweep finally work — and within a week almost all of them are gone. Frame is built around that decay rather than despite it. Everything you log comes back to you on a schedule: a few days later, then a week, then a month, timed to land just before you’d lose it. There’s nothing to revise and no flashcards to sit down with — the app works out what’s about to fade and puts it in front of you. Retention is the product, and it’s the one thing a notebook has never done.

The log you’ll actually keep

Everyone knows they should keep a training journal. Almost nobody does, because writing one up after an hour of getting choked is the last thing you want to do. Frame removes the friction: talk for thirty seconds in the car — “started in closed guard, hit a scissor sweep to mount, got swept, tapped to an armbar” — and it transcribes on-device and turns it into a structured session you just confirm. Then it reads your game back to you: the positions you live in, the subs you land, the ones that keep catching you, and hands you one or two specific things to hunt next session. A plan, not a feed.

Fig. 01 — Recall, timed to the forgetting curve

The app wears your belt

Frame is built for the belt journey and it shows it — the whole app takes on the colour of your rank and changes with you as you’re promoted. It runs offline-first and keeps your training history yours: saved the instant you log it, private, exportable, never sold. The goal underneath every decision is simple and hard: make the progress you can’t feel visible, so the belts who’d otherwise quit have a reason to come back for the next stripe — not more features, but more people still on the mat a year from now.
