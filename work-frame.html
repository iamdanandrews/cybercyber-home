The work
04 / FRAME

Frame is a native iOS journal for Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu. I designed the identity and built a product that turns a short voice log into a session record, then brings techniques back for recall. The core loop is capture, organisation and return: record the session, find the technique, then revisit it before training.

The brief
Capture the session while it is fresh. Make it useful later.

A journal only works if it is easy to keep. Frame starts with a short voice log and builds a training history that can be searched, revisited and practised from.

1For the individual

Your sessions, techniques and recall prompts in one place, with a growing record of the training you have done.

2For the room

A gym’s curriculum can become a block in the app. Your own logs show which techniques you have covered.

Start with what someone can remember.

I made a short voice note the main entry point. A practitioner can describe the session in their own words, then keep a structured record of the class, techniques and rolling.

The design separates capturing a session from organising it. The Log screen gives those records a consistent shape without requiring a long form after training.

The native interface uses belt colours, condensed headings and a clear card hierarchy. Home, Log and Game connect the everyday record to the material being learned.

Fig. 01 · Frame · onboardingThe native onboarding introduces quick session logging.

Organise the history around positions.

A chronological log answers what happened. The Game view answers where a technique belongs. I used positions as the organising structure, with sweeps, submissions and escapes visible before opening a card.

Recall prompts and the syllabus extend that record between sessions. The same recorded technique can be found through its session or its position in the game.

Fig. 02 · Frame · positions and techniquesThe native Game view groups techniques by position, with sweeps, submissions and escapes visible on each card.

Make the record worth returning to.

The Log screen keeps training in chronological order. Each session has a date and a summary, so the record can be found again without remembering which technique was covered.

I kept this view separate from the position-based Game view. One preserves the session; the other organises what was learned.

Fig. 03 · Frame · training recordThe current native Log screen keeps each training session in a dated history.

Mat hours, sessions, techniques and game flows offer different routes through the same record. Community and Inbox support the gym connection; the personal journal remains the centre of the product.
