The work
01 / GRANITE

Granite is a strength coach for athletes whose sport isn’t lifting, built to run entirely on your phone. Not a plan you follow, but an engine that reasons — writing your lifting around what you actually did to your body that week, and rewriting it as things change. It runs on fixed rules, not guesswork, and it stays on-device: every decision it makes can be traced, tested, and trusted. The engine has since outgrown the app — it now also runs Kaido Health, a rehab platform for clinics with its own case study.

The problem
A plan you cannot follow is worth nothing.
The week movesLife firstWork, sleep, travel, a niggle. The week you planned is rarely the week you get.
So the plan breaksMiss twoFixed programmes have no answer for a missed session, so people improvise or stop.
And nobody explainsNo reasonApps change your numbers without saying why, so there is nothing to trust or argue with.

One mechanism underneath all three: training adapts to the person, or the person quietly stops training.

Fig. 01 · Granite · the weekly rewriteMiss a few sessions, and it eases the week back — lighter, shorter, still complete — so you keep training instead of quitting, and don’t get hurt trying to catch up to where the plan says you should be.
Fig. 02 · Granite · the decision, closerThe same screen at reading distance. Every change is a decision you can trace back — the old number struck through, the new one next to it — because a coach you can’t interrogate is just a spreadsheet.

1For the person training

You do not need more discipline, you need a week that survives contact with your life. Granite rewrites it and tells you what changed.

2For the people who prescribe

Coaches and clinicians cannot stand behind a black box that will not show its reasoning. An engine that follows fixed rules can be inspected, tested and argued with — which is why it now also runs Kaido Health, in clinics.

A coach, not a plan

Most training apps hand you a fixed plan and hope you follow it. Granite reasons instead — taking your goals, the days you actually have, your injuries, and the load you carry off the platform — and builds a week that fits your real life.

When something changes it rewrites, and tells you why.

Fig. 03 · Granite · say this, the plan changesOne sentence about a sore shoulder, and the week changes around it — the exercise that would aggravate it swapped out, protective work added in. You keep training through the niggle instead of stopping, and nothing has to be re-entered by hand.

The engine is the product

Granite runs on fixed coaching rules, not AI guesswork. Every plan is consistent, every decision traceable, and the whole system testable against real coaching principles rather than hoping a black box got it right.

Accountable intelligence beats impressive intelligence when it’s your body on the line.

Fig. 04 · Granite · reasoning at every timescaleEvery session opens with why it exists today, inside a 12-week arc that builds and eases off on its own schedule — so you’re always working toward something, instead of guessing what to do next or grinding until you break.

On-device by decision

Running locally was the harder path, and it made the product sharper: an engine that can’t lean on a server has to do its thinking right there, the instant you need it.

That constraint is the craft. Spotting a plateau, checking a week’s volume against the evidence — analysis most apps would phone home for — happens instantly.

Fig. 05 · Granite · reading the whole picture, on-deviceIt catches a plateau before you’d notice it yourself, and makes sure no muscle group is quietly under-trained — so a stall gets fixed in week one, not month three of wondering why you’ve stopped improving.

The ledger · kept / cut
The product is the decisions we refused to skip.

+Kept

  • A rule-based engine you can trace
  • On-device — no account, no cloud
  • A weekly rewrite that explains itself

−Cut

  • An AI black box guessing your plan
  • Cloud sync and sign-in
  • Streaks, badges, a feed to scroll
One we killed

We almost shipped cloud sync in week three — it would have made onboarding one tap and the demo look cleaner. We killed it because the moment your training history touches our servers, “on-device and accountable” stops being an architecture and becomes a marketing line. The harder path was the product.

Scope. What is public here is the consumer app. The engine underneath it was built to serve more than one reader — and those surfaces stay off the site until they ship. The architecture is the asset; publishing how it decides is not.
